# Priority matrix and example SLA targets

Priority comes from impact and urgency. Replace the labels and times with your organization's service-level agreement.

| Impact \ Urgency | High | Medium | Low |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| High (many users, critical service) | P1 Critical | P2 High | P3 Medium |
| Medium (a team or important user) | P2 High | P3 Medium | P4 Low |
| Low (one user) | P3 Medium | P4 Low | P4 Low |

| Priority | Example | First response | Target resolution | Update frequency |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| P1 Critical | Company-wide email outage, site network down | 15 minutes | 4 hours | Every 30 minutes |
| P2 High | Department cannot reach a key application | 30 minutes | 8 business hours | Every 2 hours |
| P3 Medium | One user cannot print or install an approved app | 4 business hours | 3 business days | Daily |
| P4 Low | Peripheral request or how-to question | 1 business day | 5 business days | On milestones |

Definitions
- **Impact**: how many people and how critical the service is.
- **Urgency**: how quickly the business needs it fixed (deadline, work stopped or degraded).
- **First response**: first meaningful human contact, not an auto-acknowledgment.
- Pause the SLA clock only for documented reasons, such as waiting for the user or a vendor.
